## Critical Caveats

1. **Consonant-class matching is broad.** We match at the level of
   consonant features (labial, dental, velar, etc.), not specific phonemes.
   This increases sensitivity but also false positives. A match between
   Egyptian /nfr/ (N-P-R) and LA /na-pa-ra/ (N-P-R) could be coincidental.

2. **No vowel information.** Egyptian writing omits vowels entirely, and
   LA sign values are uncertain for most vowels. Our class-based matching
   intentionally ignores vowels, which is appropriate for the evidence but
   further reduces specificity.

3. **Five consonant classes cover most LA signs.** P, T, K, S, N, R, W, V
   means that random 3-class sequences are common. A 3-sign match has a
   baseline probability of ~1/7³ = 1/343 for any given position under
   uniform distribution (actual distribution is non-uniform).

4. **No directionality claim.** Even genuine matches cannot distinguish
   Egyptian→Minoan vs Minoan→Egyptian vs shared substrate borrowing.

5. **This is a text search, not linguistic proof.** No phonological regularity,
   semantic verification, or historical context has been applied.

## Method

- **Vocabulary**: 88 Egyptian trade/commerce terms from Middle/Late Egyptian
- **Corpus**: 1220 Linear A inscriptions, 4232 syllabogram tokens
- **Mapping**: Egyptian consonant → consonant class (P/T/K/S/N/R/W/G)
  LA syllabogram value → consonant class (based on phonetic onset)
- **Search**: contiguous subsequence matching of consonant-class strings
- **Filter**: ≥3 contiguous signs (consonant classes)
- **Context**: commodity logogram (GRA, OLE, VIN, etc.) ±3 sign positions
- **Dual-source**: conventional AB values AND Phase 8 expanded values searched
