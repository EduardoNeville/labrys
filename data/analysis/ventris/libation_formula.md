# Phase 11, Avenue 7 — The Libation Formula (the Rosetta-fragment)

**Status:** 🔥 DISCOVERED — the closest thing Linear A has to a fixed text

## The finding

The corpus contains a **recurring fixed formula** on the stone libation
vessels (the famous IOZa/KOZa/PKZa series from Iouktas, Kophinas, Arkalochori —
sacred mountain sites). The opening formula repeats across ~12 inscriptions:

```
AB 08  AB 51  AB 26  AB 85  AB 46  AB 49
```

| Formula fragment | Inscriptions | Sites |
|------------------|--------------|-------|
| AB 08 AB 51 AB 26 AB 85 AB 46 | 12+ | IOZa, KOZa, PKZa |
| AB 08 AB 51 AB 26 AB 85 AB 46 AB 49 | 11+ | IOZa2/3/7, KOZa1 |
| AB 49 AB 30 AB 30 AB 52 AB 12 | 11 | IOZa, KOZa |
| AB 10 AB 06 AB 62 AB 06 | 7 | IOZa, KOZa, PKZa |

## Why this is a Rosetta-fragment (the cascade potential)

1. **It's the closest thing to a known text.** Libation tables are ritual
   dedications. Minoan religion scholarship and the Linear B parallel
   (libation/dedication formulas in Mycenaean Greek) strongly suggest the
   formula means something like "to [deity], [offering]" or "may [x] be
   granted". The *genre* is known even though the words aren't.

2. **It's fixed and repetitive** — 12+ inscriptions, 3+ sacred sites, same
   opening. This is the repetition the failed scorer couldn't find because it
   operated on the whole corpus; the formula is a *sub-corpus*.

3. **AB 85 appears mid-formula** (AB 08 AB 51 AB 26 ‖ AB 85 ‖ AB 46 AB 49).
   The retracted "word divider" hypothesis reappears IN CONTEXT: AB 85 sits
   between `AB 08 AB 51 AB 26` and `AB 46 AB 49`, exactly where a word
   divider would sit between a verb/particle and a deity name. This is the
   *contextual* evidence the earlier positional finding lacked.

4. **Cascade path:** If the formula's internal structure can be segmented
   (e.g., `[verb/particle] [deity name] [offering]`), and if any segment
   matches a known Minoan deity name (e.g., from the Linear B pantheon like
   Diktaian Zeus, or the later Greek tradition of the Idaian cave), that
   gives phonetic values for the signs in that segment — which then cascade
   through the Kober network to resolve neighboring signs.

## What to do next

1. **Segment the formula.** Use the repeated 4-5 grams as boundaries: the
   formula has stable chunks (AB 08 AB 51 AB 26 / AB 46 AB 49 / AB 30 AB 30
   AB 52 / AB 10 AB 06 AB 62 AB 06). Each chunk is likely a word.
2. **Cross-reference with the deity hypothesis.** Minoan libation formulas
   in Linear B (e.g., the Knossos libation tablets) and the known Minoan
   pantheon (Dikte, Ida, the "Mistress of the Labyrinth" potnia) provide
   candidate names. If AB 46 AB 49 or AB 30 AB 30 AB 52 matches a deity
   name pattern, that's an anchor.
3. **Oracle-test the segmentation.** Hide a formula sign, see if the
   formula's positional consistency recovers it — the first oracle that
   operates on a *repetitive sub-corpus* rather than the whole corpus.

## Honest caveats

- The formula is ~12 inscriptions; the chunks are short (2–6 signs).
- We don't KNOW it's a dedication — it's an inference from genre and site.
- The "word divider" reading of AB 85 is still a hypothesis — but now it
  has contextual support (consistent medial position in a fixed formula),
  which the earlier retracted claim lacked.
- This is a *structural* discovery (a fixed formula exists) — the *semantic*
  interpretation (dedication, deity name) requires the deity-name match.

## Why this could cascade

A single deity-name match in the formula would:
1. Assign phonetic values to 2–6 signs (the deity name segment)
2. Those values cascade through Kober links to neighbors
3. The formula's other chunks (verb, offering) become testable against the
   known vocabulary (ku-ro, ki-ro, the commodity words)
4. The libation sub-corpus becomes a mini-Rosetta — a fixed text with a
   hypothesized meaning, exactly the anchor Ventris used

**The libation formula is the most promising farfetched avenue. It's the
closest thing to a known text, it's repetitive, it's fixed, and it sits in
a known genre (ritual dedication).**
