**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Research:** web-sourced, current state of Linear A decipherment

## 1. Why we can't cross the threshold from where we stand

The decipherment threshold requires **three things** (per the field's
consensus), and we have only partial versions of them:

| Required | Field consensus | Our state |
|----------|----------------|-----------|
| **Known language** | The underlying Minoan language is unknown — no secure family (Tyrsenian is a weak proposal, not established) | Same — no family confirmed |
| **Parallel/bilingual text** | No bilingual exists (unlike Rosetta, Behistun) | Same — no bilingual in our corpus |
| **Independent phonetic anchors** | Need external anchors (like Ventris's Cypriot syllabary) | We have LB-transfer (circular) + CM (partially deciphered) + the formula (phonetically inert) |

**The specific wall:** every phonetic value we have is LB-transfer (circular —
derived from the same evidence we'd need to verify it). The oracle proved the
scorer can't recover values from the corpus alone. The word-level morphology
shows *structure* (-RO suffix, A- prefix) but not *meaning*, because meaning
requires the language to be known — and it isn't.

**What would break the wall:**
1. A **bilingual find** (Linear A + known language) — the only true Rosetta
2. **Significantly more data** (the corpus is ~11K tokens ≈ 1 page of text;
   the new Knossos scepter is the longest single text and adds ~73 signs)
3. **A confirmed Cypro-Minoan value chain** (CM is itself undeciphered — only
   9-11 signs have plausible correspondences)

## 2. Latest archaeological finds (2024-2025)

### The Knossos Anetaki ivory scepter (the big one)
- **Find:** an elephant-ivory ring-and-handle "religious scepter" from the
  Anetaki plot / Cult Center at Knossos (excavated 2011-2017, highlighted
  2024, formally discussed 2025)
- **Importance:** carries the **longest known Linear A inscription** (~73 signs
  across two inscribed faces)
- **Status:** no accepted translation; it's a cult object, likely a
  dedication/invocation

### Other Minoan finds (2024-2025)
- **Papoura Hill, near Kastelli:** a unique ~1,800 m² circular stone complex
  (2000-1700 BCE)
- **Archanes:** a rare gate sanctuary found during 2025 excavations
- **Zominthos, Karphi, Sissi, Chrysi Island:** ongoing excavations (2024-25)
  with findings, mostly architectural/domestic

### The key point
None of the new finds are **bilingual** or contain a **known-language
parallel**. The scepter is the longest text but it's still Linear A — it adds
data, not a Rosetta.

## 3. Latest research (2024-2025)

### Computational approaches
- **"Minoan and the Machines"** (2025): a review/evaluation of computational
  approaches — **no decipherment announced**. Confirms the field sees
  computation as promising but not decisive.
- **Tamburini 2025 (Frontiers in AI):** coupled simulated annealing
  (CSA_OptMatcher) for lost scripts — applied to known scripts (Cypriot
  syllabary, etc.), not a Linear A decipherment. Interesting method, no
  breakthrough.
- **"Linear A and the Machine" (2024):** a brute-force attack paper —
  speculative, not accepted.

### Linguistic/comparative
- **Tyrsenian proposal:** still not established — no demonstrated sound
  correspondences or shared morphology with Etruscan/Lemnian/Rhaetic
- **Language family:** remains unknown; Minoan is an isolate by default
- **Cypro-Minoan:** undeciphered; ~9-11 signs with plausible correspondences
  only

### The consensus
**No accepted decipherment exists.** The field's methodological progress
(corpus digitization, computational tools — like our project) has not crossed
the threshold. The missing piece remains: a known language, a bilingual, or an
independent anchor.

## 4. What this means for our project

**We are exactly where the field is** — but with a corrected corpus, an honest
grid (59 confirmed), a mapped formula, and quantified word-level morphology.
Our position is genuinely better than most:

1. The Knossos scepter (~73 signs, longest text) — **we should incorporate it**
   if the transcription becomes available. It's the single largest new data.
2. The field's consensus confirms our oracle finding: computation alone can't
   cross the threshold; an anchor is required.
3. Our word-level lexicon + morphology is the structure ready to receive an
   anchor when it arrives.

**The honest bottom line:** the threshold is linguistic (a known language or
bilingual), not computational. Our project has done the computational work as
well as it can be done on the current corpus. The next step — new data
(especially the scepter) or a genuine anchor — is external to computation.
