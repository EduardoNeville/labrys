## Morphological structure (NEW — quantitative)

**Suffixes (2+ syllable words):** -RO (78×), -JA (65×), -RE (58×), -TE (56×),
-NA (54×), -TI (49×), -RA (48×), -RA₂ (39×), -TA (37×), -RU (36×), -SI (36×)

**Prefixes:** A- (155×), KU- (89×), I- (80×), JA- (63×), DA- (60×), SA- (60×)

**Key patterns:**
1. **-RO suffix family**: KU-RO (total) / KI-RO (owed) — the accounting
   nominalizer. First syllable carries the semantic distinction.
2. **Root + suffix variation**: SI-DA-RE / SI-DA-TE / SI-DA-RO — the same
   root with 3 different suffixes (productive suffixation)
3. **KU- prefix family**: KU-RO, KU-PA₃-NU, KU-NI-SU, KU-PA, KU-RE...

## What this enables

1. **Testable morphology hypotheses**: -RO as nominalizer, A- as a prefix
2. **Word families**: roots with suffix variants reveal derivation
3. **The formula words fit**: ja-sa-sa-ra-me (ja- prefix + -me suffix),
   si-ru-te, u-na-ka-na-si — all in the word inventory

## What's STILL stopping us (honest)

The word values are **LB-transfer** — morphology reveals *structure* (that
-RO is a suffix, that A- is a prefix) but not *meaning* (what they mean).
The circularity the oracle proved still holds: without independent phonetic
anchors, we see patterns but can't assign meanings.

**BUT** — the word-level data is a genuine advance: it gives the project a
**lexicon with morphological structure** that any future anchor (bilingual
find, new inscription) can plug into. The structure is the asset.
