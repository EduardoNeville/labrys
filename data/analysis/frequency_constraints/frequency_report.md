## Methodology

1. **Frequency envelopes**: For each CONFIRMED consonant class and vowel row,
   we compute mean ± 2σ frequency ranges. Any candidate whose observed
   frequency falls outside this envelope is flagged.
2. **Kober grid series**: Signs in the same Kober series share either
   consonant or vowel. If an UNCERTAIN sign's series partners all share
   a consonant class (or vowel), and the candidate matches neither,
   it is eliminated.
3. **Kober triples**: Three-sign patterns with shared preceding/following
   context provide additional constraints from CONFIRMED partners.
4. **Typological priors**: Very high-frequency signs (>200 occurrences)
   cannot belong to typologically rare consonant classes (sibilants,
   semivowels).

## Caveats

- Frequency envelopes are based on 58 CONFIRMED signs — a small sample.
- CV slot statistics are sparse; many slots have <2 CONFIRMED signs.
- The Kober grid series classification into boundary-flexible,
  medial-dominant, and neutral is based on positional analysis,
  not phonetic certainty.
- Frequency alone is a WEAK constraint compared to phonetic or
  comparative evidence. Eliminations here should be treated as
  suggestive, not definitive.
- AB 62 (freq=285), AB 66 (freq=308), AB 85 (freq=274) are extreme
  outliers even compared to common CONFIRMED signs. They may be
  grammatical morphemes rather than content phonemes, which would
  invalidate the frequency-class approach.
- The task brief mentions AB 62 appearing 437 times, matching
  triple_patterns.csv total_occ. Our DB count using
  sign_type='syllabogram' gives 285. The triple_patterns column
  counts at a coarser level. We use the DB count as authoritative.
- The medial-dominant Kober series spans all consonant classes and
  all vowels among its CONFIRMED members, providing zero class-level
  constraint for its UNCERTAIN members (AB 49, AB 52, AB 56, etc).
- For signs with zero corpus occurrences, no frequency constraint is
  possible. Other evidence sources must carry the full burden.
